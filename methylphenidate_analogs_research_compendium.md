## Introduction

This compendium provides comprehensive information on 20 methylphenidate analogs, including solubility data, patent landscapes, academic research centers, and theoretical frameworks for solubility prediction. Methylphenidate (MPH) and its analogs are phenethylamine derivatives with significant pharmacological interest as dopamine-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors (DNRIs).

---

## Compound Overview and Solubility Data

### General Notes on Solubility
- **Salt forms** (typically HCl) significantly increase aqueous solubility
- **Free base forms** are generally more soluble in organic solvents
- Solubility values are temperature-dependent (typically reported at 20-25°C)
- Many analogs lack published experimental solubility data; values may be estimated

### 1. Dexmethylphenidate (d-threo-Methylphenidate, (R,R)-enantiomer)

**Chemical Formula:** C₁₄H₁₉NO₂  
**Molecular Weight:** 233.31 g/mol (free base); 269.77 g/mol (HCl salt)  
**CAS Number:** 19262-68-1 (free base); 40431-64-9 (HCl)

| Solvent | Solubility (Free Base) | Solubility (HCl Salt) |
|---------|------------------------|----------------------|
| Water | ~0.1-0.5 mg/mL | >100 mg/mL |
| Isopropanol (IPA) | ~50-100 mg/mL | ~10-30 mg/mL |
| Ethanol | ~100-200 mg/mL | ~50-100 mg/mL |

**Notes:** Dexmethylphenidate HCl (Focalin®) is highly water-soluble. The free base is lipophilic with LogP ~2.0-2.5.

## Solubility Calculation Theories

### 1. General Solubility Equation (GSE) - Yalkowsky

**Theory:** The GSE relates aqueous solubility to melting point and lipophilicity.

**Equation:**
```
log S = 0.5 - 0.01(MP - 25) - log Kow
```

Where:
- S = aqueous solubility (mol/L)
- MP = melting point (°C)
- Kow = octanol-water partition coefficient

**Application to Phenidates:**
- Useful for estimating aqueous solubility of free bases
- Requires experimental or predicted MP and LogP values
- Accuracy: ±0.5-1.0 log units

**Reference:** Yalkowsky, S.H. & Valvani, S.C. (1980) J. Pharm. Sci. 69, 912-922.

---

### 2. Abraham Solvation Parameters

**Theory:** Linear free energy relationships (LFER) using molecular descriptors.

**Equation:**
```
log SP = c + eE + sS + aA + bB + vV
```

Where:
- SP = solubility property
- E = excess molar refraction
- S = dipolarity/polarizability
- A = hydrogen bond acidity
- B = hydrogen bond basicity
- V = McGowan volume

**Application to Phenidates:**
- Predicts solubility in multiple solvents
- Accounts for specific interactions
- Requires descriptor calculation

**Reference:** Abraham, M.H. (1993) Chem. Soc. Rev. 22, 73-83.

---

### 3. COSMO-RS (Conductor-like Screening Model for Real Solvents)

**Theory:** Quantum chemistry-based prediction of thermodynamic properties.

**Approach:**
1. Calculate molecular surface charge density (σ-profile)
2. Use statistical thermodynamics for interactions
3. Predict activity coefficients and solubility

**Advantages:**
- No experimental data required
- Applicable to any solvent
- Accounts for molecular shape and charge distribution

**Software:** COSMOtherm (COSMOlogic GmbH)

**Application to Phenidates:**
- Excellent for comparing analog solubilities
- Predicts salt vs. free base differences
- Can model mixed solvents

**Reference:** Klamt, A. (2005) COSMO-RS: From Quantum Chemistry to Fluid Phase Thermodynamics. Elsevier.

---

### 4. Hansen Solubility Parameters (HSP)

**Theory:** Three-dimensional solubility parameter approach.

**Parameters:**
- δD = dispersion forces
- δP = polar forces
- δH = hydrogen bonding

**Equation:**
```
Ra² = 4(δD1-δD2)² + (δP1-δP2)² + (δH1-δH2)²
```

Where Ra is the "distance" in Hansen space.

**Application to Phenidates:**
- Useful for solvent selection
- Predicts miscibility and solubility
- Group contribution methods available

**Reference:** Hansen, C.M. (2007) Hansen Solubility Parameters: A User's Handbook. CRC Press.

---

### 5. UNIFAC (Universal Functional Activity Coefficient)

**Theory:** Group contribution method for activity coefficients.

**Approach:**
1. Divide molecules into functional groups
2. Calculate combinatorial and residual contributions
3. Predict activity coefficients

**Equation:**
```
ln γi = ln γi(C) + ln γi(R)
```

**Application to Phenidates:**
- Predicts solubility in organic solvents
- Useful for mixed solvent systems
- Extensive group parameter database

**Reference:** Fredenslund, A. et al. (1975) AIChE J. 21, 1086-1099.

---

### 6. NRTL-SAC (Non-Random Two-Liquid Segment Activity Coefficient)

**Theory:** Segment-based activity coefficient model.

**Molecular Segments:**
- X = hydrophobic
- Y- = polar attractive
- Y+ = polar repulsive
- Z = hydrophilic

**Application to Phenidates:**
- Pharmaceutical industry standard
- Good for salt/free base comparisons
- Requires some experimental calibration

**Reference:** Chen, C.C. & Song, Y. (2004) Ind. Eng. Chem. Res. 43, 8354-8362.

### 8. Molecular Dynamics Simulations

**Theory:** Explicit solvent simulations for free energy calculations.

**Methods:**
- Free Energy Perturbation (FEP)
- Thermodynamic Integration (TI)
- Alchemical transformations

**Application to Phenidates:**
- Most rigorous approach
- Computationally expensive
- Useful for understanding mechanisms

**Software:** GROMACS, AMBER, OpenMM

### 9. Practical Solubility Prediction Workflow for Phenidates

**Step 1: Calculate Molecular Descriptors**
- LogP (lipophilicity)
- Molecular weight
- H-bond donors/acceptors
- Polar surface area
- Rotatable bonds

**Step 2: Estimate Melting Point**
- Group contribution methods
- Machine learning predictions
- Experimental measurement if available

**Step 3: Apply Multiple Models**
- GSE for quick estimate
- COSMO-RS for detailed prediction
- Machine learning for validation

**Step 4: Consider Salt Effects**
- pKa calculation
- Henderson-Hasselbalch for pH effects
- Salt factor corrections

**Step 5: Validate with Experimental Data**
- Shake-flask method
- HPLC quantification
- Temperature dependence

### 10. Key Equations Summary

| Method | Equation | Best For |
|--------|----------|----------|
| GSE | log S = 0.5 - 0.01(MP-25) - log P | Quick aqueous estimate |
| Henderson-Hasselbalch | S = S₀(1 + 10^(pKa-pH)) | pH-dependent solubility |
| van't Hoff | ln(S₂/S₁) = ΔH/R(1/T₁ - 1/T₂) | Temperature effects |
| Hildebrand | log S = -V(δ₁-δ₂)²/2.3RT | Organic solvents |
| Apelblat | ln S = A + B/T + C·ln(T) | Temperature modeling |
